1 · The problem

A reactor where the product destroys itself

0:00 – 1:00 
A --k1--> B --k2--> C
          ^          ^
       product     waste

B is the product and the feedstock for the waste reaction. Every second B spends in the reactor, some of it becomes C. Run too short and A never converts; run too long and B is destroyed.

What we predict: yield of B from five operating knobs — feed rate, concentration, inlet temperature, reactor length, jacket temperature.

Why it matters industrially The rigorous simulation is, in the problem statement's own words, "computationally expensive and too slow for real-time plant optimization." An engineer changing a setpoint cannot wait for a boundary-value solve.

2 · Chemical understanding

Two activation energies, and their ratio

1:00 – 2:00
ReactionActivation energy
Desired A → B43.16 kJ/mol
Waste B → C250.07 kJ/mol
E₂/E₁ = 5.79 Heat accelerates destruction ~6× more steeply than formation. The rate constants cross at 449.9 K, inside the observed 351.6–548.0 K.
But it is not a temperature ceiling Yield depends on k·τ, not k. Run hot with a short residence time and B leaves before it degrades: 49 training rows sit above the crossover and one reaches 75% yield. The rule is joint in (T, τ) — above 449.9 K, every extra unit of residence time costs selectivity.

Opposite signs, near-equal magnitude — they cancel. Not thermal neutrality: forcing a₁ = a₂ = 0 costs 8.27 vs 3.66 train. Likewise corr(log τ, yield) = +0.061 is an interior optimum, not irrelevance.

Presenter notes · 60 s Highest-weight slide. Three claims, each a number: the ratio, the joint (T, τ) rule, the a₁/a₂ cancellation. If a reaction engineer challenges the crossover as an operating limit, agree immediately and give the 75%-yield row — we tested that ourselves. The concentration argument is most likely to be probed: the tempting answer ("first-order, CA₀ cancels") is incomplete, and the next one ("thermally neutral") is measurably wrong.

We recovered the reactor, not a regressor

Why this approach for this problem 150 rows cannot support a flexible model at a sharp cliff, and a tree cannot extrapolate past its outermost split. An ODE can — and its parameters are quantities a process engineer can check against known kinetics.

Three choices, each measured

  • Noise-averaged prediction at σ = 1.67 K, fixed a priori from an errors-in-variables fit — never tuned on the CV that judges it. It makes the training fit worse (3.656 → 3.764) and cross-validation better (6.358 → 5.928) on all three seeds. A blur that helps held-out error is evidence of input noise, not overfitting.
  • A 13% ExtraTrees component, weight derived not tuned: error correlation ρ = 0.070 between the two models gives a variance-optimal w* = 0.887; we ship 0.87.
  • Validation. Repeated 10-fold plus cold-start folds refit from scratch (5.98) — two independent protocols agreeing.
A limit of our own protocol Leave-one-seed-out underprices hyper-parameter selection, because all seeds re-partition the same 150 rows. Measured at row level, selection costs +0.371 RMSE — so we read our 5.671 as a lower bound.

5 · Failure analysis & application

Where it fails, and how you would tell

4:15 – 5:00

If this started predicting badly tomorrow

SourceSymptom
ChemicalFouling raises U · catalyst degradation shifts k₁ · feed change moves CA₀ · operation outside 351.6–548.0 K
Sensor / dataNear the cliff a 2 K temperature error becomes a ~20-point yield error. Median measured input offset 1.67 K; 12 of 150 rows resist any offset within ±25 K
ML / modelThe cliff: 29% of rows carry 91% of squared error. Our blend also lifts 6 dead-reactor predictions above 5% yield (max 11.5)
Known limits, stated plainly 150 rows, one reactor geometry. Tube diameter is constant across every row, so it folds into the fitted pre-exponentials — a different tube needs a refit, not a rescale. And the cliff rows stay genuinely hard: we tested 8 independent improvement strategies after submission and none cleared our adoption bar.

The diagnostic — and it works only because the model is mechanistic

Refit the seven parameters and read which one moved A black-box model tells you the prediction is wrong. It cannot tell you which piece of the plant changed.
What movesDiagnosis
U aloneheat-transfer fouling
k₁ alonecatalyst degradation
CA₀ path onlyfeed composition change
Nothing moves, errors risesensor drift or unmodelled phenomenon

That is the practical case for recovering the reactor instead of approximating it: the model is not just a predictor, it is an instrument.
