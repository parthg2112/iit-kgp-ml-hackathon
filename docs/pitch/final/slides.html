1 · The problem

I.II.III.IV.V.

A reactor where the product destroys itself

B is the product and the feedstock for the waste reaction. Every second B spends in the reactor, some of it becomes C. Run too short and A never converts; run too long and B is destroyed.

What we predict

Yield of B from five operating knobs: feed rate, concentration, inlet temperature, reactor length, jacket temperature.

Why it matters industrially

The rigorous simulation is, in the problem statement's own words, "computationally expensive and too slow for real-time plant optimization." An engineer changing a setpoint cannot wait for a boundary-value solve.

2 · Chemical understanding

I.II.III.IV.V.

Two activation energies, and their ratio

E₂/E₁ = 5.79

Heat accelerates destruction ~6× more steeply than formation. The rate constants cross at 449.9 K, inside the observed 351.6–548.0 K.

But it is not a temperature ceiling

Yield depends on k·τ, not k. Run hot with a short residence time and B leaves before it degrades: 49 training rows sit above the crossover and one reaches 75% yield. The rule is joint in (T, τ): above 449.9 K, every extra unit of residence time costs selectivity.

Opposite signs, near-equal magnitude: they cancel. Not thermal neutrality: forcing a₁ = a₂ = 0 costs 8.27 vs 3.66 train. Likewise corr(log τ, yield) = +0.061 is an interior optimum, not irrelevance.

Presenter notes · 60 s Highest-weight slide. Three claims, each a number: the ratio, the joint (T, τ) rule, the a₁/a₂ cancellation. If a reaction engineer challenges the crossover as an operating limit, agree immediately and give the 75%-yield row; we tested that ourselves. 3 · ML methodology

I.II.III.IV.V.

We recovered the reactor, not a regressor

dCA/dz = -k1·CA
dCB/dz =  k1·CA - k2·CB
dT/dz  =  a1·k1·CA + a2·k2·CB + U·(T_jacket - T)

7 physical parameters. Fixed functional form. No architecture search. The form came from the chemistry, so there was nothing to search.

Model Protocol RMSE
Best tree ensemble (+ physics features) 10f-CV 16.37
Physics ODE 10f-CV 6.36
Physics ODE training fit 3.66

Three choices, each measured

  • Noise-averaged prediction at σ = 1.67 K, fixed a priori from errors-in-variables, never tuned on the CV that judges it. Training worse (3.656 → 3.764), cross-validation better (6.358 → 5.928) on all three seeds: input noise, not overfitting.
  • A 13% ExtraTrees component, weight derived not tuned: error correlation ρ = 0.070 gives a variance-optimal w* = 0.887; we ship 0.87.
  • Validation. Repeated 10-fold plus cold-start folds refit from scratch (5.98): two independent protocols agreeing.

A limit of our own protocol

Leave-one-seed-out underprices hyper-parameter selection: all seeds re-partition the same 150 rows. At row level that costs +0.371 RMSE, so 5.671 is a lower bound.

Why this approach for this problem

150 rows cannot support a flexible model at a sharp cliff; a tree cannot extrapolate past its outermost split. An ODE can, and an engineer can check its parameters against known kinetics.

Rank 13 of 400. The metrics disagree, and that is the finding: RMSE / MAE = 3.41 against 1.25 Gaussian, 1.41 Laplace. Too high for broadly mediocre prediction.

≈47 of 50 rows predicted to about 1 yield-point. Two or three cliff rows missed by ~50. R² 0.9061 implies a test spread of 36.0 vs 38.3 in training, and a two-sample classifier confirms the match (AUC 0.500).

What we got right, and what we did not

We pre-registered the mechanism: a few cliff rows carry nearly all squared error. The score confirms it, but we underestimated the magnitude. Our bootstrap put RMSE in [2.94, 8.10]; 11.04 sits outside. MAE we called: 3.24 observed, [1.74, 4.03] predicted. None of our 150 training rows missed by over 40 points; the test set produced two or three.

5 · Failure analysis & application

I.II.III.IV.V.

Where it fails, and how you would tell

If this started predicting badly tomorrow

Source Symptom
Chemical Fouling raises U · catalyst degradation shifts k₁ · feed change moves CA₀ · operation outside 351.6–548.0 K
Sensor / data Near the cliff a 2 K temperature error becomes a ~20-point yield error. Median measured input offset 1.67 K; 12 of 150 rows resist any offset within ±25 K
ML / model The cliff: 29% of rows carry 91% of squared error. Our blend also lifts 6 dead-reactor predictions above 5% yield (max 11.5)

The diagnostic, and it works only because the model is mechanistic

Refit the seven parameters and read which one moved

A black-box model tells you the prediction is wrong. It cannot tell you which piece of the plant changed.

What moves Diagnosis
U alone heat-transfer fouling
k₁ alone catalyst degradation
CA₀ path only feed composition change
Nothing moves, errors rise sensor drift or unmodelled phenomenon

Known limits, stated plainly

150 rows, one reactor geometry. Tube diameter is constant across rows, so it folds into the fitted pre-exponentials; a different tube needs a refit, not a rescale. Cliff rows stay hard: 8 strategies tested after submission, none cleared our bar. Hence recovering the reactor rather than approximating it: not just a predictor, an instrument.
